Final Project

Develop a focused, feasible GIR project with your team and present the results in Week 11.

Milestones and Assessment

  • Week 8: meet your team and develop a project idea during the in-class workshop.
  • Before your Week 9 class: submit a 1-2 page team proposal using the outline below. Each member should upload the same team proposal to Moodle.
  • Week 11 in class: give the final team presentation. Every team member should present one part.
  • Week 11 evening: submit the final project materials, including slides, code, data and other relevant materials.

Possible Project Directions

These are starting points rather than strict categories, and they can overlap.

System design

Build a small GIR workflow that connects components such as datasets, geoparsing, geocoding, indexing and ranking.

Technique

Focus on one method, for example a hybrid geoparsing workflow that combines a model with rules and gazetteer lookup.

Spatial analysis

Use GIR data or methods to investigate a geographic pattern, for example place-name ambiguity.

Evaluation

Evaluate how an existing method or model handles a geographic task, for example georeferencing place mentions.

Proposal

The proposal should be about 1-2 pages and contain the full name and student ID of every team member. Use the following five sections.

  1. Project Aim and Context: state one focused GIR question or project aim, explain why it matters, and define the scope.
  2. Data and Materials: describe the data, tools and fields you need, where they come from, and what area, period or cases you will include.
  3. Proposed Approach and Analysis: describe the main stages, explain your method choices, and say how you will check, compare or interpret the results.
  4. Expected Outcomes: explain what the team plans to produce and what the results could help you learn.
  5. Work Plan and Feasibility: assign the main tasks, identify owners and timing, and check that the plan is realistic given data access, tools, time and team skills.
Keep the scope manageable.

The goal is a clear and feasible course project. You do not need to promise a particular finding in the proposal, but the question, workflow, data access and planned outputs should be concrete enough for the team to start working.
